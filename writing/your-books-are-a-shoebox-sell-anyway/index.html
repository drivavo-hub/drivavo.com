Your books are a shoebox. Sell anyway.

What messy looks like from the buyer's side, what I can work around, and the two things I can't.

Bill Henry · October 25, 2026

The most common reason an owner gives me for not being ready to sell isn't the business. It's the books. He has a bookkeeper who comes in once a quarter, or a spouse who does it at the kitchen table on Sunday night, or a box. He knows a buyer is going to ask for numbers, he's embarrassed by what he has, and so he puts the whole conversation off for another year. Another year of the same box.

I want to take that reason off the table. Over twenty years building a company from a handful of people to well over a hundred, the work that mattered most was collections and systems, which is a polite way of saying I've spent a long time making sense of money after the fact. Messy isn't a deal killer. Messy is a Tuesday.

What messy looks like from my chair

A profit and loss statement printed last spring for the tax preparer and not touched since. A truck payment, a fishing boat, and the kids' phones all running through the business. Cash jobs that may or may not have made it into the deposit. A customer list that is a stack of invoices, a route sheet in the truck, and a notebook by the phone. Three years of tax returns that show a business making less than it obviously makes, because the owner's accountant was doing his job.

I expect all of it. A twelve-person shop that has been running for fifteen years was never going to look like a public company, and if it did, I'd wonder what else had been dressed up.

Most of it I can work around. Personal expenses are the easiest. I'll add them back. If the fishing boat is really a fishing boat, it comes out of the expenses and into the earnings you get paid on. I'm not going to argue with you about the truck.

Returns that understate the business are next. The returns are where I start, but they aren't where I stop. If the bank statements show more money coming in than the returns do, we walk through the gap line by line. The gap is normal. The explanation is what I'm buying.

No real profit and loss at all? I'll build one. Twelve months of bank statements, the invoices, the payroll reports, and a few hours with whoever writes the checks, and I'll have a picture I trust more than most of the pretty ones I get handed.

The software you never set up isn't a reason to wait either. I put systems in after closing. That's what I do.

The two things I can't

Two things I can't work around, and I'd rather tell you now than find them in diligence.

The first is revenue that doesn't tie to the bank. If you tell me the business does one number and the deposits say a smaller one, the smaller one is the business. Cash work is real, and so is the fact that some of it never makes it to the bank. But I can't pay for money I can't see, and no buyer spending his own money will. If a lot of your revenue is cash, start depositing it now. A year of deposits that match your story will do more for your price than anything else on this list.

The second is customers who only exist in your head. When I ask who your regulars are and the answer is "I know every one of them," that's true, and it's also the problem. I'm not buying your memory. I'm buying the route. If there's no list, no schedule, no record of who gets serviced when and what they pay, then when you walk out the door the customers walk out with you.

This one is fixable, and it's cheap. A spreadsheet is fine: name, address, phone, what you do for them, how often, what they pay, when you were last there. Start it this week. By the time you're ready to sell, it will be the most valuable document you own.

Sell anyway

If your books are a shoebox, you are not disqualified. You are normal. The clean-books shop is the exception, and I pay a little more for it because I have to hold back a little less. But I'd rather buy a good business with bad books than a bad business with good ones, and I can tell the difference.

Get the cash into the bank. Get the customers onto a list. Then call me, box and all.
